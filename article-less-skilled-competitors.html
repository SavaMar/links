Visibility · 5 min read

WHY LESS SKILLED PEOPLE
ARE GETTING THE CLIENTS
YOU DESERVE

Mari Miavka · April 2026 · Moutier, Switzerland

You've been doing this for fifteen years. They've been doing it for three.

They're fully booked. You're chasing the next project.

They raised their prices last year. You've kept yours the same for two.

They speak at conferences. You attend them.

And you know — you know — that you are better. More experienced. More skilled. The difference in quality between what you deliver and what they deliver is not even close.

So why is this happening?

THE MARKET DOESN'T
CHOOSE THE BEST.

I know this is uncomfortable to read. But it's true, and nobody else is going to say it directly.

The market chooses the most visible. The most credible-looking. The one whose presence, before any conversation has happened, already communicates: this person is the real deal.

Your competitor figured this out — maybe consciously, maybe accidentally. They invested in how they show up before you even speak. Good photos. A clear, consistent message. Content that builds trust in the scroll. A visual presence that signals their level before a client ever reaches out.

You didn't. You've been too busy doing excellent work.

"In the first fifteen seconds, a potential client is not evaluating your skill. They're evaluating the signals you send about your skill."

Expertise is invisible until it's demonstrated. And online, it's demonstrated through what people see — not what they eventually experience when they hire you.

WHAT "LOOKING THE PART"
ACTUALLY MEANS

It doesn't mean expensive. It doesn't mean perfect. It means: your visual presence communicates what you actually are.

Consistent visuals. One clear aesthetic thread across your website, LinkedIn, Instagram. When someone encounters you in three different places, they feel the same thing each time. That consistency is read subconsciously as reliability. As someone who is serious about what they do.

A clear message. Not "I help clients achieve their goals." Not "I offer bespoke solutions." Something specific. Something that makes the right person stop and think: this is exactly what I need. Your competitor has this. It took them about six months to find it. You have twice the experience — yours would take a third of the time.

Content that does the trust work before the first call. Your competitor is probably not writing better content than you could. They're just showing up consistently. Three LinkedIn posts a month, a photo that looks like a person who takes their work seriously, and a profile that explains clearly who they help and what changes. That's it. That's the whole edge.

"Your competitor isn't winning because they're better. They're winning because, before anyone speaks to either of you, they already look like the obvious choice."

THIS IS NOT ABOUT
BECOMING SOMEONE ELSE

The instinct, when you realise visibility is the gap, is to start performing. To put on a version of yourself that matches what you see the successful people doing. To copy the aesthetic, the tone, the format.

That's the wrong direction entirely.

The goal is not to look like your competitor. The goal is to finally look like you — the version that already exists in the room when you're doing your best work. The version your best clients describe when they recommend you. The version that has fifteen years of specific, hard-earned experience behind it.

That version hasn't been translated into anything visual yet. That's the whole problem. And it's fixable.

WHAT CHANGES
WHEN YOU FIX IT

I've seen it happen. I've been through a version of it myself.

When the outside finally matches the inside, the referral quality changes. The people who reach out arrive already understanding what you're worth. The negotiation feels different. The price conversation feels different. You stop losing clients to people you know are worse than you.

It doesn't happen because you became better at your work. You were already good. It happens because you stopped being invisible.

The gap between you and your competitor is not your skill level. It never was. It's the gap between who you actually are and what people can see before they decide.

That gap has a cost. You've been paying it for years.

You don't have to keep paying it.
